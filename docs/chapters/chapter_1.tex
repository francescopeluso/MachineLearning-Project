\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

This report describes how we designed, trained and selected the model we deliver
for the segmentation of solar filaments in H-alpha images, and also which
hypotheses we tried and had to reject along the way.

Two facts shaped most of our decisions. First, \textbf{the annotations are subjective}:
each observation was traced by up to three experts who agree with one another only
to a Dice of $0.62$, so part of the error we measure is expert disagreement rather
than a modelling error. Second, \textbf{two different scores are in play}: the project work
assignment is evaluated on the Mean Dice, while the Kaggle challenge ranks its
leaderboard by Panoptic Quality (PQ). The delivered configuration is optimised for
the former; the latter is studied separately, in Section~\ref{sec:kaggle}.

The main contributions are:

\begin{itemize}\itemsep0pt
    \item a U-Net with a pre-trained ResNet18 encoder reaching \textbf{validation
          Dice 0.6815}, above inter-annotator agreement, inside the optional GPU
          budget (3.04~GB in training, 2.50~GB at inference);
    \item an evaluation pipeline that re-implements the challenge's own PQ and is
          verified against the organisers' code to $3\times10^{-8}$, so every
          validation number is directly comparable with the leaderboard;
    \item a post-processing stage whose four hyperparameters are grid-searched
          jointly on the validation set -- worth more than most training changes
          and costing no training time at all;
    \item a falsification record: fifteen training runs and a dozen post-hoc
          mechanisms measured and, for the most part, rejected, together with a
          measured noise floor below which a validation gain cannot be believed.
\end{itemize}

Section~\ref{sec:design} how the experiments were planned,
