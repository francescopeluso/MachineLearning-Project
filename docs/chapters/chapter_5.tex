\section{Second campaign: instances}
\label{sec:second-campaign}

The first campaign ended with a diagnosis -- what limits the score is instance
recognition -- and the second attacked it, keeping the same discipline: one hypothesis
per run, falsified results reported with the same care as confirmed ones. From
Experiment~7 on, the internal test split is folded into training ($+22\%$ data),
legitimate exactly because it is never used to choose anything.

\subsection{The ceiling is annotator disagreement, and it binds SQ}

Factorised, the shipped point's $\mathrm{PQ} = 0.4264$ is $\mathrm{SQ} = 0.668 \times
\mathrm{RQ} = 0.638$ over 1144 ground-truth instances (TP~712, FP~376, FN~432). Across
every capacity, resolution and architecture tried in thirteen experiments, SQ moved
inside $0.66$--$0.68$ and no further -- against a matched-pair agreement between human
annotators of $0.59$. The conclusion is uncomfortable but useful: \textbf{SQ is a
property of the annotation process, not of the model}. Every point of PQ still on the
table goes through RQ: finding more of the filaments annotators marked, predicting
fewer they did not.

\subsection{Where the error actually is}

At the shipped operating point, the 432 false negatives split into 244 \emph{pure
misses} (not one predicted pixel on the instance), 159 \emph{near misses} (a blob is
there but stays at IoU $\le 0.5$, median predicted/true area $0.62$ -- the model
systematically under-segments) and 29 \emph{splits}; the 376 false positives into 128
\emph{hallucinations}, 239 on annotated but unmatched filaments, and 9 \emph{merges}.
Instance recall falls sharply with size, from $0.34$ below 500~pixels to $0.75$ above
5000 (Figure~\ref{fig:predictions}); at the recall-leaning balanced point the smallest
bucket rises to $0.475$ at the cost of 62 extra false positives, and that is where the
diagnosis behind the next campaign was read. Two families dominate -- small-instance
recall and under-segmentation; split/merge errors are under $4\%$ of the total, which
is why no split/merge machinery was ever built.

Two caveats qualify these counts, and both were checked. First, the organisers have
confirmed in the competition forum that the annotations are \emph{incomplete} --
visible filaments may be missing -- so part of the 128 hallucinations are plausibly
real filaments no annotator marked; measured on the multi-annotator files, even blobs
whose probability peaks above $0.85$ are marked by only $63\%$ of annotators on
average, which is why the confidence gate, not a recall push, sits at the PQ optimum.
Second, the judging committee reads the \emph{many-to-one / one-to-many} distribution
alongside the score (Figure~\ref{fig:distributions}): here the matching structure is
nearly one-to-one -- of 1144 annotated instances, 900 are touched by at least one
prediction and only 67 by more than one; of 1088 predictions, 960 touch an annotated
filament and only 20 touch more than one.

\subsection{Mechanisms measured and rejected}
\label{sec:falsified}

Several plausible mechanisms were evaluated and discarded -- most of them cheaply, on
the \emph{frozen} probability maps of the final model, reproduced bit-exactly on a
second machine so that any instantiation idea could be measured in minutes without a
training run.

\textbf{Topology and cascade machinery.} clDice at $1536$, where the soft skeleton is
finally meaningful, still loses $0.013$ PQ (Exp.~9); a detect-then-refine cascade loses
$0.025$ (Exp.~11). Both attack fragmentation, which the error analysis had already
shown to be marginal. \textbf{Soft consensus targets} (Exp.~12) turn annotator
disagreement into the label; calibration barely moves and PQ drops $0.033$.
\textbf{Scale jitter} (Exp.~10) costs $0.019$. \textbf{Per-component local thresholds}
($\tau_c = \alpha \cdot \mathrm{peak}_c$, or a quantile of the component's own
probability mass) should fix under-segmentation without over-fattening correct
components; measured, they lose to the plain global threshold, because every surviving
component peaks near $1.0$ and the local cut collapses into a global one.
\textbf{Per-component dilation} (nearest-component distance transform, so merges are
impossible) loses at every radius -- $\delta = 1$~px already costs $0.03$ PQ -- because
near misses are not eroded copies of the truth but differently shaped, typically
missing a faint tail. \textbf{Ensembling probability maps} loses twice: with the weaker siblings
(Exps.~9, 10, 12), whose miscalibration dilutes the marginal detections, and even with
the near-equal, oppositely-biased Exp.~15 -- a $+0.003$ aggregate gain that scores
\emph{below} the single model under cross-selection. The emerging rule: on 169
validation entries, any gain below $\approx +0.005$ cannot be told from selection
noise. And \textbf{Mask~R-CNN} was rejected on paper:
it rebuilds each mask from a $28 \times 28$ grid inside an axis-aligned box, an
upsampling of $\sim\!25\times$ per axis for structures whose defining property is being
a few pixels wide and hundreds long.

\textbf{Post-hoc merging of split filaments} was bounded and then falsified. An oracle
that merges, with ground-truth knowledge, exactly the prediction pieces that would flip
a split into a match gains $+0.017$ PQ -- but only 18 of its 29 merge groups flip
cleanly, because the annotators disagree on connectivity itself. No blind mechanism
captures that ceiling: relabelling two instances as one when they are connected at a
low probability threshold (no pixel added, so Dice is untouched) gains $+0.004$ in
aggregate, yet under cross-selection the two validation halves pick opposite ends of
the parameter grid and the transferred gain is $-0.006$ -- selection noise, not signal;
merging by spatial proximity loses at every useful radius. Low-probability bridges
cannot tell one broken filament from two nearby ones.

The extent and merge mechanisms failing identically is the finding of the campaign:
the under-segmentation and the fragmentation both live in the model's probability
support, not in the thresholding. No post-processing can claim them; only training
changes can.

\subsection{Validation versus leaderboard}
\label{sec:gap}

The public score is systematically below validation, and the gap decomposes into
measured parts: an early submission was produced by a non-final recipe (worth
$\sim$0.02); ordinary generalisation to a different annotator draw ($\sim$0.026,
measured once on the internal test split); and composition ($\le 0.01$ -- PQ
stratified by annotator multiplicity, GONG station and year varies within counting
noise, and the test set's station/year mix matches validation). The submission path
itself was audited clean: disjoint components by construction, RLE conventions matching
the organisers' scorer, zero components off the solar disk.
