\section{Experimental strategy}
\label{sec:design}

\subsection{Hypotheses and decision criteria}

The experimental plan was defined around a limited set of hypotheses. Each hypothesis
was associated with a measurable comparison and with a decision that could be taken
from the result. Table~\ref{tab:plan} summarises this structure.

\begin{table}[ht]
  \centering
  \caption{Hypotheses used to guide the experimental work.}
  \label{tab:plan}
  \small\begin{tabular}{L{0.29\linewidth}L{0.31\linewidth}L{0.29\linewidth}}
    \toprule
    Hypothesis & Test & Decision criterion \\
    \midrule
    Pre-trained features are useful on the limited labelled set.
      & Compare a U-Net trained from scratch with a ResNet18 encoder.
      & Retain transfer learning only if validation Dice improves. \\
    Fragmentation is mainly a loss-function problem.
      & Introduce Tversky and clDice while keeping the low input resolution.
      & Retain the specialised loss only if both Dice and PQ improve. \\
    Thin filaments are lost during downsampling.
      & Increase the input from 512 to 1024 and then 1536 pixels.
      & Prefer the scale that improves object recognition at acceptable cost. \\
    Full-disk context and native detail produce different errors.
      & Compare resized full frames with native-resolution crops.
      & Evaluate Dice, PQ and the type of instance errors jointly. \\
    High-resolution training can satisfy the memory targets.
      & Replace a physical batch of 2 with two accumulated micro-batches.
      & Accept the change if quality is retained and measured memory decreases. \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

During the study, 22 configurations were run. Section~\ref{sec:results} reports only
the eight numbered runs that provided evidence for the hypotheses above; the
full-resolution U-Nets and YOLO detector experiments are compared separately in
Section~\ref{sec:heavy}.

In the first transfer-learning run, the learning rate was also changed, while higher
input resolutions required different
memory settings. The results therefore support practical design decisions, but the
entire score difference cannot always be attributed to one variable.

All final scores are recomputed at the native image resolution, and output parameters
are selected only on validation data.

In some experiments, the training seed was changed a couple of times while keeping the
split fixed. The resulting scores remained nearly unchanged, suggesting limited
variability for the tested configurations.

\subsection{Alternatives not retained}

The main alternatives considered and then rejected were:

\begin{itemize}
\setlength{\itemsep}{0.1em}
\item \textbf{Alternative losses:} topology-oriented, soft and instance-balanced variants gave no stable gain.
\item \textbf{Scale jitter:} inconsistent gains.
\item \textbf{Post-processing:} detect-then-refine and component merging increased complexity or fusion risk.
\item \textbf{ResNet34 encoder:} more memory without a Dice gain.
\item \textbf{Detectors:} higher PQ, lower Dice and higher cost.
\end{itemize}

These results guided the choice of input scale and final architecture.
