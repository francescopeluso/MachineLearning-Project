\section{From probabilities to a submission}
\label{sec:operating}

Training produces a probability map; four decisions turn it into the mask that is
scored, and together they are worth more PQ than any single training change. All are
selected on validation only, and \emph{jointly}, because they interact -- tuning the
threshold first and the filters afterwards locks in a worse point (on Experiment~5,
$0.389$ sequential against $0.401$ joint).

\begin{itemize}\itemsep0pt
    \item the \textbf{threshold} that binarises the probability map;
    \item \textbf{morphological closing}, which reconnects a filament the network
          split in two, converting two FP and one FN into one TP;
    \item a \textbf{minimum component size}, deleting the tiny spurious components of
          Section~\ref{sec:experiments} that each cost a full false positive;
    \item a \textbf{confidence gate}: a component survives only if its probability
          peaks above the gate somewhere. The threshold alone does two jobs at once --
          deciding \emph{which} blobs exist and \emph{how fat} they are -- so raising
          it to kill weak blobs also thins every real filament. The gate takes over
          the existence decision and leaves the outlines where the threshold drew them.
\end{itemize}

Test-time augmentation over the eight square symmetries averages eight probability
maps and is kept behind a flag; the threshold must then be re-selected on the averaged
maps, otherwise TTA looks harmful when it is not.

\subsection{Scoring what the leaderboard scores, where it scores it}
\label{sec:native}

Two things had to be right for a validation number to mean anything. \textbf{What}: the
official PQ of Section~\ref{sec:metrics}, re-implemented and verified to
$3\times10^{-8}$ against the organisers' code. On the union of two or three annotators
an image carries about eleven filaments against under seven for any single one, so a
model tuned against the wrong definition systematically over-predicts.
\textbf{Where}: the submission is written at $2048^2$, but early operating points were
selected at training resolution -- a protocol mismatch, since a minimum size tuned at
$1024$ means something else at $2048$ and the number of connected components is not
preserved by resizing a mask at all. The fix is to enlarge the \emph{probability} map
to native resolution and only then threshold, post-process and score. Measured on
Experiment~5, selecting at native resolution against the official metric is worth
$+0.020$ Dice and $+0.006$ PQ over the recipe that would otherwise have shipped
(Table~\ref{tab:native}) -- for free, with no retraining.

A last refinement came late and cost nothing: the leaderboard reports PQ and nothing
else, but the recipe had historically been frozen on the \emph{balanced} objective
$\mathrm{Dice} + \mathrm{PQ}$. Re-selecting the final model's point on pure PQ, with a
second, finer threshold pass around the coarse optimum
(Figure~\ref{fig:threshold-search}), moves it from $0.4194$ to $\mathbf{0.4264}$
(threshold $0.48$, closing $1$, minimum size $384$, gate $0.97$). Cross-selection --
choosing the point on one half of the validation files and scoring it on the other --
puts the overfitting cost of the finer grid at only $0.0024$, with both halves
independently selecting the same threshold and gate: the refinement is real, not grid
noise.
