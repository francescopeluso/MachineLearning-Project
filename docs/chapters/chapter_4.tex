\section{Model and training}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Architecture}

The model is the U-Net of Section~\ref{sec:background} with a \textbf{ResNet18
encoder pre-trained on ImageNet} (Figure~\ref{fig:architectures}): with fewer than
500 training observations, learning edges and textures from scratch wastes the data
budget. The classification head is discarded and the four convolutional blocks feed
the U-Net decoder through the skip connections. A one-time algebraic rewrite folds
the RGB stem and the ImageNet normalisation into an exactly equivalent
single-channel convolution, so grayscale images enter directly at a third of the
input bandwidth. The architecture then stays fixed for the whole study: one
variable per experiment is what makes the comparisons meaningful.

Three alternatives were considered and rejected for stated, testable reasons.
\textbf{Instance-segmentation architectures} such as Mask R-CNN~\cite{maskrcnn}
rebuild each mask from a $28 \times 28$ grid inside an axis-aligned box -- a
$\sim\!25\times$ upsampling for objects whose defining property is being a few
pixels wide and hundreds long -- and elongated diagonal filaments produce heavily
overlapping, mostly-background boxes, the worst regime for anchor matching. The
claim was later tested empirically rather than left on paper: a
YOLOv8x-seg~\cite{yolo} detector trained on the same split reaches a competitive
instance score (PQ $0.426$) but a clearly worse Dice ($0.632$ against $0.68$),
confirming the choice for the assignment's score. \textbf{Larger encoders and
transformers}: across every capacity and resolution tried the quality of matched
masks never moved (Section~\ref{sec:error}), so capacity is not the binding
constraint, while the data budget argues against hungrier models. \textbf{Non-neural
pipelines} (thresholding plus morphology): H-alpha contrast varies strongly between
observations, and a global photometric rule cannot separate filaments from sunspots
and limb darkening.

\subsection{Loss, optimisation and implementation}

The loss is an equally weighted combination of binary cross-entropy and soft
Dice~\cite{vnet}: Dice is immune to the $0.4\%$ positive rate but says nothing
about confidence, while BCE keeps the probabilities \emph{calibrated}, which is
what makes the threshold search of Section~\ref{sec:operating} well behaved --
experiment 3 shows what happens when that calibration is lost. Augmentation uses
the eight symmetries of the square, which are label-preserving because filaments
have no canonical orientation, plus photometric jitter for the varying H-alpha
contrast. Optimisation is AdamW~\cite{adamw}, batch size 2, at most 20 epochs, with
early stopping on validation Dice. The checkpoint is selected on \textbf{Dice}, not
PQ: PQ depends strongly on the threshold and post-processing, which are tuned
afterwards on the same validation set, so selecting weights with it would optimise
two coupled quantities at once.

Two implementation choices make the study affordable. Images and masks are decoded
\emph{once} into memory-mapped uint8 caches -- the whole ground truth at $1024^2$
takes 150~MB instead of 1.2~GB -- so an epoch only reads bytes instead of decoding
JPEGs and rasterising polygons. Training runs in mixed precision with gradient
checkpointing at the higher resolutions, and peak GPU memory is \emph{measured} at
the step that actually peaks, never estimated from parameter counts.
