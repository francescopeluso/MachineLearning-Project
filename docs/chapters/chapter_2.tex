\section{Model and pipeline}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{A U-Net with a pre-trained ResNet18 encoder}

A classification CNN compresses an image into one prediction; segmentation needs one
per pixel, so the spatial detail the encoder discards has to be put back. U-Net does
this with a symmetric encoder--decoder and \textbf{skip connections} that copy each
encoder stage to the decoder stage of the same resolution
(Figure~\ref{fig:architectures}, left). For thin, elongated, irregular structures the
skips are not a refinement -- they are what keeps a filament from dissolving in the
bottleneck. The network emits one logit per pixel; a sigmoid turns it into a
probability, and a threshold selected on validation into the binary mask.

The baseline (Experiment~1) uses a from-scratch encoder with 1.9M parameters. From
Experiment~2 onwards the encoder is a \textbf{ResNet18 pre-trained on ImageNet}
(Figure~\ref{fig:architectures}, right, without its classification head), fine-tuned
end to end: with fewer than 500 training observations, learning basic edges and
textures from scratch wastes the data budget. The decoder stays the same, so the
comparison isolates the encoder. The architecture is then held fixed for the rest of
the study on purpose -- one variable per experiment is what makes the comparisons mean
anything.

One free optimisation is worth a paragraph. Feeding grayscale to an ImageNet encoder
normally means replicating the channel three times and normalising with the RGB mean
$m_c$ and standard deviation $s_c$. Writing out the stem pre-activation for a grayscale
image $g$ shows the redundancy:
%
\begin{equation}
\sum_{c} W_c * \frac{g - m_c}{s_c}
= \Bigl(\sum_{c} \frac{W_c}{s_c}\Bigr) * g \;-\; k,
\end{equation}
%
where $k$ is a per-output-channel constant that the batch norm immediately after
absorbs into its running mean. The three-channel stem is therefore replaced by an
exactly equivalent \emph{single-channel} one: the pre-trained weights are fully
preserved, the input tensor shrinks threefold, and normalisation disappears from the
data pipeline.

\subsection{Loss and training protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}

The loss is an equal mix of binary cross-entropy and soft Dice. Each term does a job
the other cannot: Dice is immune to the $0.4\%$ positive rate but says nothing about
confidence, while BCE keeps the probabilities \emph{calibrated}, which is what makes
the threshold search of Section~\ref{sec:operating} well behaved. Experiment~3 shows
what happens when that calibration is lost.

The protocol is identical in every run. The split is on \textbf{file names}, never on
annotation records -- the same JPEG appears up to three times in the COCO file, once
per annotator, and splitting on records would put one copy in training and another in
validation, inflating every score. Seed 22 gives 494/106/107 observations (806/169/179
records), written to \texttt{splits/split\_seed22.csv}; the internal test split is
consulted once, after everything is frozen. Augmentation uses the eight symmetries of
the square (filaments have no canonical orientation) plus photometric jitter.
Optimisation is AdamW, batch size 2, at most 20 epochs, early stopping on validation
Dice with patience 8. The checkpoint is selected on \textbf{Dice}, not PQ: PQ depends
strongly on the threshold and post-processing, tuned afterwards on the same validation
set, and selecting weights with it would optimise two coupled quantities at once.

\subsection{Engineering for iteration speed and memory}

Decoding a $2048^2$ JPEG and rasterising its polygons costs more than a forward and
backward pass; done inside the dataset, that price recurs every epoch. All images and
masks are therefore decoded \emph{once} into memory-mapped uint8 arrays (masks
bit-packed: the whole ground truth at $1024^2$ takes 150~MB instead of 1.2~GB), and
every run reuses the cache. Training runs in mixed precision with gradient
checkpointing at the higher resolutions; peak GPU memory is measured at the moment a
step actually peaks, not estimated from parameter counts. Against the assignment's
optional budget (5~GB training, 4~GB test), inference stays well inside at 2.5~GB;
training measured 3.7~GB at $1024^2$ and 5.2~GB for the full-frame $1536^2$ recipe of
the final model -- a windowed-native configuration (Experiment~13,
Section~\ref{sec:second-campaign}) brings the same step to 3.0~GB, though it lost PQ
and was not kept. Validation probability maps are stored as uint8 --
the threshold grid steps far coarser than $1/255$ -- which is what makes the joint
operating-point search of Section~\ref{sec:operating} affordable at native resolution.
